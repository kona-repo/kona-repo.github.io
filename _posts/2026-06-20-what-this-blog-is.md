This is a working log, in public.

I'm studying the security canon from scratch — starting with CISSP — and at every step asking one question: what does this principle actually mean once it's inside a vehicle?

I already hold Japan's Registered Information Security Specialist (RISS) qualification and work in automotive cybersecurity, but I'm taking CISSP cold, domain by domain. The textbook gives the generic, "correct" answer. A car — with its 15-year hardware, its safety-critical functions, and regulations like UN-R155 and ISO/SAE 21434 — often forces a different one. The gap between those two is what I want to write down.

So each post takes one idea from the security canon and holds it up against the reality of a vehicle: where it holds, where it bends, and where it simply breaks.
